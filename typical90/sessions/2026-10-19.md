**Set it up precisely.** Fix the anchor `a_0` (that's the boundary condition). On the remaining `N−1` coordinates define

$$D:\quad (a_0,a_1,\dots,a_{N-1}) \;\mapsto\; \big(a_0,\; b_0,\dots,b_{N-2}\big),\qquad b_i = a_{i+1}-a_i$$

$$P:\quad \big(a_0,\,b_0,\dots,b_{N-2}\big) \;\mapsto\; (a_0,a_1,\dots,a_{N-1}),\qquad a_i = a_0 + \sum_{j=0}^{i-1} b_j$$

**`P ∘ D = id`.** For any `i ≥ 1`,

$$a_0 + \sum_{j=0}^{i-1} b_j \;=\; a_0 + \sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\big(a_{j+1}-a_j\big) \;=\; a_0 + \big(a_i - a_0\big) \;=\; a_i$$

Everything cancels pairwise: `-a_0` with `+a_0`, `-a_1` with `+a_1`, and so on down the chain. **That cancellation is the entire mechanism.**

**`D ∘ P = id`.** For any `i ≤ N−2`,

$$a_{i+1}-a_i \;=\; \Big(a_0+\sum_{j\le i} b_j\Big) - \Big(a_0+\sum_{j\le i-1} b_j\Big) \;=\; b_i$$

Both directions are the identity, so `D` and `P` are mutually inverse linear maps. Nothing is lost — which is exactly why you could throw `A` away in 064 and rebuild any elevation from `B` if you ever wanted to.

**The matrix picture, if you want to see *why* they look like opposites.** Let `S` be the shift (`(Sa)_i = a_{i-1}`, `(Sa)_0 = 0`). Then

$$D = I - S, \qquad P = I + S + S^2 + \cdots + S^{N-1}$$

and indeed `(I−S)(I+S+…+S^{N-1}) = I − S^N = I`, since `S^N = 0`. So "prefix sum" is the geometric series of the shift, and "difference" is its `1 − x`. Everything else follows: `D` is bidiagonal with `1`s on the diagonal and `−1`s below; `P` is lower-triangular with all `1`s. One is the other's inverse, so **every statement about prefix sums has a dual statement about differences** — which is why your instinct that they're "complements in disguise" was literally correct.
